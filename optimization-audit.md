# Optimization audit notes

Supporting evidence for the [backend optimization matrix](todo.md#matrix), audited on **September 9, 2026**. The numbers used in these notes are **stable technique IDs**, referring to the index below, **not positions in the matrix sorted by estimated impact**. The matrix uses **A–K** for backend columns and **1–46** for displayed rows, so **E12** identifies the Javapurs ADT-field cell. These coordinate row numbers are separate from the audit IDs below.

## Estimated impact order

The matrix orders techniques by their estimated overall performance importance for our five backends and altbak's core workloads. Broad removal of computations, closure calls and dictionary dispatch comes first, followed by native representations, allocation/ownership improvements, and more localized transformations. TAST and TypeApp appear beside the specializations they enable; their benefits are not independent speedups. Self-tail-call support also has a feasibility role for deep recursion, even when the remaining loop is already fast.

The [README baselines](README.md#core-stresstest-benchmark-results-pure-computational) inform the weighting: Red-Black Tree dominates much of the total runtime, while Lazy Evaluation is also substantial in Rust and Sharpurs. This keeps ADT representation/reuse, closure specialization and thunk/invariant work prominent. The order combines breadth with potential impact; it is not solely a sort by one test's largest native ratio, by current missing features, or by implementation effort. Narrow techniques can rank much higher on a matching workload.

This is an engineering estimate, not a measured per-pass ranking. The baselines compare complete pipelines, so they cannot isolate each technique's contribution. Gains overlap, neighboring positions are approximate, and the most useful next change still depends on the backend and its current generated code.

## Exact scope of the rows

- **01–03**: optimize known values and implementations. Green in 02 covers the bindings/imports handled by the optimizer, not a promise to remove every unused export in the program. Yellow in 03 distinguishes these reductions from systematically eliminating all user-defined dictionaries.
- **04–06**: three separate capabilities. Reading TAST does not mean using every type; substituting `forall a` at a call does not mean creating a standalone `f_Int` function. Row 06 requires this **function cloning**, beyond regions that become monomorphic after inlining. A `Monomorphize.purs` module present in a PBO that is not invoked receives no credit.
- **07**: **automatic** uncurrying of ordinary functions. Explicit `FnN`/`EffectFnN` support alone is insufficient; constructors are covered separately in 26. A worker may still use `Object`, `obj`, or `Value`: see 09.
- **08–10**: a native operator, a primitive local, and a primitive ADT field are three different outcomes. Gray in 09 applies to ABIs that already pass their language's native scalars without adding an `Integer`/`Double` box. Their physical numeric storage is managed by the runtime. Red in 10 does not deny the existence of ADT classes, tuples, or records: it indicates that payloads are not specialized by type.
- **11**: a singleton, shared literal, or immediate tag. Coverage limited to entirely nullary enums leaves the cell yellow for mixed ADTs.
- **12–14**: record layout, field types, and update strategy. A layout with fixed fields does not guarantee unboxed numeric payloads. Native JS objects already have named properties; a pass replacing an artificial Map with a record is unnecessary there, hence gray in 12. V8 decides their machine layout. Gray in 13 applies the same distinction as 09 to targets with native/tagged scalar values; it does not mean their records or access paths are optimal. PHP `stdClass`, Scheme association lists, and Erlang maps still have alternative layouts to explore in 12.
- **15–17**: self TCO, mutual TCO, and counter recognition are separate. Scheme and Erlang provide native tail calls, so an additional backend trampoline is unnecessary, hence gray in 15/16. This does not remove the cost of their closures. Yellow for F# depends on the emitted tail-call forms and then F#/.NET; yellow for Wasm depends on `return_call` and compatible representations. Row 17 requires transforming the recursive pattern, not merely emitting a `for` in an FFI or collection intrinsic.
- **19–21**: frontend CSE mainly shares synthetic dictionary applications; an invariant cache avoids repeating a computation during an invocation; thunk fusion avoids building/forcing the chain. These three techniques are not interchangeable. Neither an internal compiler cache nor a global constant initialized once satisfies row 20.
- **22**: targeted coverage of higher-order functions (HOFs)/closures through known values and inlining, specialized representations, or private workers. Mechanisms differ by column and are detailed below. No yellow cell means general defunctionalization of all closures. CPS combinator fusion achieved through inlining is counted here, not as the dedicated thunk pass in row 21.
- **23**: `[]int64`, `int[]`, `Vec<i64>`, or an equivalent selected by the compiler. A speculative V8 optimization, a PHP array of zvals, or handwritten FFI is insufficient. Wasm is yellow for its explicit primitive arrays; ordinary `Array Int` is not generally specialized.
- **24 / 27 / 28**: reusing a unique object, removing a local cell, and moving/borrowing a value instead of cloning a reference are distinct. Gray in 28 applies to targets where the backend does not emit such explicit clones/reference counts. PHP's zval management belongs to the engine; in Go, the presence of a historical `Rc` field does not prove it is used.
- **25**: erasure of the `newtype` data wrapper, so the value has the representation of its payload. An exported identity constructor or a residual call to it does not add a data wrapper; eliminating those calls is a separate simplification. Generic scalar boxing belongs to 09, not to an extra `newtype` envelope.
- **29**: scrutinee fusion here means eliminating an ADT that is constructed and then immediately destructured: reducing a `case` over a known constructor, or distributing the consumer into the producer's branches (*case-of-case*). Yellow covers these local forms, including after inlining; it does not promise fusion across a producer call that remains opaque. This row is separate from thunk fusion in 21.
- **30–33**: eta reduction removes a forwarding lambda; scalar replacement removes a record/array allocation; update coalescing merges writes or forwards reads; decision trees share/reorder tests on inputs that can remain unknown. These are distinct from automatic arity changes (07), residual record copying (14), and constructed-scrutinee elimination (29). Wasm's 31/32 coverage is limited to known records; opaque update chains remain. PBO coverage is guarded by expression forms and usage counts, and Sharpurs only consumes accepted optimized paths.
- **34**: explicit, recognized and saturated `runFnN` / `runEffectFnN` calls for **positive arities 1–10**, including unknown first-class callees, must use a genuine native multiargument call. Supported `mkFnN` lambda spines must produce that ABI. Arity zero, every opaque adapter construction, and ST APIs are outside this row's green scope. An `UncurriedApp` IR node that becomes unary calls is insufficient. This row does not imply unboxed parameters or automatic ordinary-function uncurrying.
- **35–38**: surviving class-specific dictionary structs, ordinary `data` wrapper erasure, nullable sums, and value-struct returns remove different representation costs. Row 35 requires a layout derived from class declarations with method fields; ordinary JS properties alone do not satisfy it. Row 36 excludes semantic `newtype` erasure and local SROA. Row 37 excludes host-selected niches. Row 38 removes the outer payload-constructor box on selected result paths; its fields may still contain boxed `Value`s. Nullary immediates already belong to 11, and primitive payload fields to 10.
- **39**: an explicit capture analysis chooses a static function representation for a **local lambda**, avoiding an allocated closure environment. Ordinary named globals and possible host allocation elimination are insufficient. Bridges can still allocate, hence Rust's yellow.
- **40–41**: lowering a recognized library loop/traversal to statements differs from combining several collection constructions into one. Row 41 includes dynamic constituents/tails, excluding mere concatenation of two constant literals. Neither row promises general map/filter/fold producer-consumer fusion. Handwritten FFI/library loops alone receive no credit.
- **42–43**: returning the original constructor when an update is already equal is safe without mutation or unique ownership (24). Separately, converting a local recursive lambda into a worker with captures as explicit parameters can remove captures from the worker; it does not guarantee removal of its first-class bridge closure. Rust's workers can remain lexically nested native `fn` items; Wasm lifts supported groups to top level.
- **44–45**: keeping an existing non-tail case continuation once is different from extracting an already duplicated suffix from branches. Wasm explicitly introduces `LetJoin`. The other targets naturally retain one continuation via a value-producing expression, IIFE, or shared result slot, hence gray in 44; IIFEs may still cost a closure/call. This is not credit for an explicit join-point optimization or PBO's TCO join roles. Row 45 requires actual common-tail factoring.
- **46**: list-specific lowering to target cons cells/list operations. Purescm handles standard `Data.List.Types`; purerl handles explicit `Erl.Data.List` APIs. Green is within those respective APIs, not a claim that purerl specializes every standard PureScript List. Go's general node-pointer/nullable ADT representation belongs to 37 and is not a separate list intrinsic.

## Provenance and methodology

The `bin/*/run` scripts, dependencies actually selected, entry points, invoked passes, generic fallback paths, existing tests, and previously generated outputs were cross-checked. The second pass expanded the matrix from **29 to 46 techniques** and checked all eleven pipelines against the same candidates. The [coverage inventory](#coverage-inventory) maps the inspected pass families to rows, including mechanisms grouped under existing rows and candidates not established. This is systematic coverage of the audited pipelines, not a claim to inventory every possible compiler or host-runtime optimization.

Positive evidence requires an implemented mechanism, an active invocation, compatible incoming IR shapes, and a code generator that consumes the result. No benchmark or full compiler rebuild was run for this documentation change. The initial cross-backend review inspected existing tests without rerunning them. The subsequent Java review ran the existing TCO, direct-call, loop-invariant, counted-loop, Int-loop, typed-record and nullary-constructor checks against the already built compiler modules; they passed. Isolated PBO and generated-Java probes exposed the additional limitations recorded in the Java section. A targeted FSI probe also checked nullary-constructor sharing in Sharpurs's existing Release DLL, as recorded below. These checks establish specific behavior, not measured performance gains or complete frontend-to-backend coverage.

## Shared frontend and PBO evidence

- **Limited CSE, row 19**: [Make.hs](../purescript/src/Language/PureScript/Make.hs), `optimizeCoreFn`, then serialization in [Make/Actions.hs](../purescript/src/Language/PureScript/Make/Actions.hs). The [official 0.15.15 selection](https://github.com/purescript/purescript/blob/v0.15.15/src/Language/PureScript/CoreFn/CSE.hs#L369) targets simple `IsSyntheticApp` expressions: dictionaries, superclasses, and `IsSymbol`. This is not general CSE across all arithmetic computations. Wasm additionally has `SShared` sharing and CAFs.
- **PBO, rows 01–03, 18, 22, 25**: [Builder](../purescript-backend-optimizer-javapurs/src/PureScript/Backend/Optimizer/Builder.purs), `buildModules` → `toBackendModule`; [Convert](../purescript-backend-optimizer-javapurs/src/PureScript/Backend/Optimizer/Convert.purs), selection of used bindings and `IsNewtype`; [Semantics](../purescript-backend-optimizer-javapurs/src/PureScript/Backend/Optimizer/Semantics.purs), evaluation, inlining, `shouldUnpackCtor`, `shouldUnpackRecord`, and let floating. Known aggregates are genuinely eliminated, but this does not constitute general uniqueness analysis.
- **TypeApp, row 05**: in the same `Semantics`, `evalTypeApp` and `instantiateNeutralType` substitute types under `ForAll`, and `evalExternFromImpl` actually uses the instantiation before the value arguments. [TypeApp tests](../purescript-backend-optimizer-javapurs/test/typeapp.mjs). Ignoring a **residual** TypeApp in the final code generator therefore does not mean the entire pipeline ignored it.
- **Operations and effects, rows 08 and 18**: [Semantics/Foreign](../purescript-backend-optimizer-javapurs/src/PureScript/Backend/Optimizer/Semantics/Foreign.purs) recognizes functions including `intAdd`, `intMul`, `bindE`, and `pureE`, then builds `PrimOp` / `EffectBind` / `EffectPure`. The target code generator then emits the operators and statements: the contribution is shared, hence `(PBO)`.
- **TCO, row 15 for Phpurs/Gopurs/Javapurs, 15–16 for ES**: [Codegen/Tco](../purescript-backend-optimizer-javapurs/src/PureScript/Backend/Optimizer/Codegen/Tco.purs) analyzes tail calls and loop roles; the cited code generators use that analysis. Emitting loops or the dispatcher remains specific to the backend. Purust and Sharpurs use their own paths for this capability.
- **Saturated construction, row 26**: `evalApp` in `Semantics` transforms a fully applied `NeutCtorDef` into `NeutData`, then reification produces `CtorSaturated`. The code generators consume this form; Sharpurs does so in its kernels. Its separate generic path retains curried calls for constructor heads wrapped in `TypeApp`, as detailed in the Sharpurs section.
- **Attribution boundaries**: row 04 describes the code generators' representation choices based on the TAST produced by the frontend; CSE in row 19 also comes from the frontend. Merely carrying this information does not earn a `(PBO)` label. In row 07, `shouldUncurryAbs` is disabled in the audited local PBO (`Nothing`): our five backends implement their own workers. Gopurs monomorphization in 06 does, however, actually call the PBO monomorphizer, as detailed below.
- **Scrutinee fusion, row 29**: in `Semantics`, `shouldUnpackCtor` removes a constructor used only for field reads or tag tests. `shouldDistributeBranches` pushes the consumer into the branches under the guards `size <= 128`, `result == KnownNeutral`, and uses limited to accesses/tests; `RewriteDistBranchesLet`, `evalAccessor`, and `evalPrimOp` then simplify known fields and tags. These mechanisms exist in our five dedicated PBO copies, in ES 1.4.3, and in the PBO pinned by purescm. Sharpurs benefits through the selected paths described below. The [KnownConstructors03 source case](../purescript-backend-optimizer-gopurs/backend-es/test-snapshots/src/Snapshot.KnownConstructors03.purs) constructs a `Just`/`Nothing` and then pattern-matches it; the [reference output](../purescript-backend-optimizer-gopurs/backend-es/test-snapshots/snapshots-out/Snapshot.KnownConstructors03.js) contains only an `if` returning the final strings, without that intermediate ADT. This snapshot illustrates the shared PBO infrastructure, not a new measurement of the five backends.
- The entry points cited for each backend determine whether this result is actually emitted. Sharpurs consumes only part of it; the historical ES/purescm packages use their own PBO versions.
- **Eta, row 30**: local `Semantics.shouldEtaReduce` removes a forwarding lambda whose arguments are exactly the bound locals in order. It matches bare `Abs`/`App`/`Local` nodes and arguments; intervening `Typed` wrappers prevent this match. The rule executes on a bare-IR probe, but that alone does not establish a reachable positive case from Javapurs's current TAST input (see E32 below). The corresponding rule is also active in ES 1.4.3 and purescm's pinned PBO; it does not change the function's arity.
- **Aggregate scalar replacement, row 31**: `shouldUnpackRecord`, `shouldUnpackArray`, `shouldUnpackUpdate`, the `RewriteUnpackOp` evaluator, and `evalAccessor` replace supported aggregate uses with individual field bindings/values. The [local record-update snapshot](../purescript-backend-optimizer-gopurs/backend-es/test-snapshots/snapshots-out/Snapshot.InlineReferenceRecordUpdate.js) reduces a constructed update to `fn({}).c`. Coverage differs by version: [ES 1.4.3 UnpackArray01](https://github.com/aristanetworks/purescript-backend-optimizer/blob/5e7643253ebc9db16f3c9ab702fa756bd3e41b80/backend-es/test/snapshots-out/Snapshot.UnpackArray01.js) removes its intermediate array, while the local fork's corresponding snapshot retains array expressions. The local array snapshot is therefore not cited as a successful elimination.
- **Update coalescing, row 32**: `evalUpdate` merges nested `NeutUpdate` fields with outermost-write precedence; `evalAccessor` forwards the written field or follows the base. Supported [Record.Builder/Record.Unsafe foreign semantics](../purescript-backend-optimizer-gopurs/src/PureScript/Backend/Optimizer/Semantics/Foreign.purs) expose related forms. This can simplify writes on an unknown base; the remaining update uses the target's record-update strategy, such as copying fields or structural sharing.
- **Pattern decision trees, row 33**: `Convert.buildCaseTreeFromRows`, `normalizeCaseRows`, `chooseNextPattern`, and decomposition select test columns and share constructor tests across alternatives. [CaseHeuristics source](../purescript-backend-optimizer-gopurs/backend-es/test-snapshots/src/Snapshot.CaseHeuristics.purs) and [output](../purescript-backend-optimizer-gopurs/backend-es/test-snapshots/snapshots-out/Snapshot.CaseHeuristics.js) demonstrate testing a useful argument first. Product/record decomposition avoids unnecessary tags. The rating does not claim globally optimal trees.
- **Explicit arity, row 34**: `Semantics/Foreign` recognizes positive FnN/EffectFnN APIs and constructs `UncurriedAbs`/`UncurriedApp` and effect forms. Only Rust, PHP, Go, ES and purescm consume these as native N-ary first-class paths, with the restrictions documented below. Java/Sharp recognition or ordinary direct-call workers do not establish this ABI.

## sharpurs

- **01–05, 18**: [Main](../sharpurs/sharpurs/src/Main.purs) and [CodeGen](../sharpurs/sharpurs/src/Sharpurs/CodeGen.purs) select ADT/Thunk/Int kernels and [Optimized](../sharpurs/sharpurs/src/Sharpurs/Optimized.purs) wrappers. The general path still traverses **the original TAST declarations**, rather than all the optimized PBO declarations. The mere presence of `buildModules` therefore does not justify green ratings everywhere.
- **07–10**: [DirectCall](../sharpurs/sharpurs/src/Sharpurs/DirectCall.purs) supports monomorphic NonRec bindings and exactly saturated qualified calls; `obj` workers/wrappers remain. [IntKernel](../sharpurs/sharpurs/src/Sharpurs/IntKernel.purs), [IntArithmetic](../sharpurs/sharpurs/src/Sharpurs/IntArithmetic.purs) and [AdtLayout](../sharpurs/sharpurs/src/Sharpurs/AdtLayout.purs) create native paths for supported Int/Bool/closed ADTs. External or polymorphic fields can cause the kernel to be rejected; the fallback is a DU with `obj` fields.
- **11 (A39)**: nullary DU cases use F#'s shared representation, including cases in mixed ADTs. An FSI probe against the existing Release `altbak.pub-sharpurs/run/bak/sharp/output/Main/bin/Release/net8.0/Program.dll`, without rebuilding, returned `ReferenceEquals = true` for two constructions each of `Data_Maybe_Nothingusd_Ctor`, `Test_RBTree_Eusd_Ctor`, and `Test_RBTree_Rusd_Ctor`. See the [generic Maybe](../altbak.pub-sharpurs/run/bak/sharp/output/Main/Data.Maybe.fs) and [native RBTree](../altbak.pub-sharpurs/run/bak/sharp/output/Main/Test.RBTree.fs) declarations. Green credits the selected DU representation's sharing, implemented by F#; it does not claim that payload constructors are allocation-free.
- **26 (A24)**: recognized saturated constructors become direct DU construction or a registered native factory, including selected PBO paths. Coverage is partial: `CodeGen.flattenApp` does not traverse `ExprTypeApp`. The original TAST for `Data.Tuple.swap` contains `App(App(TypeApp(TypeApp(Var Tuple, ...), ...), b), a)`, which falls back to a curried constructor lambda and two `sharpurs_apply` calls in the [generated Tuple module](../altbak.pub-sharpurs/run/bak/sharp/output/Main/Data.Tuple.fs). Hence yellow, with `(PBO)` retained for the accepted optimized paths.
- **12–14, 23–24**: records use `Map<string,obj>`, updates use `Map.add`, and generic arrays are boxed. `Map.add` provides structural sharing rather than copying every field. Red for record updates (14 / A20) denotes the absence of an update specialized to a fixed field layout; it does not assert a full record copy on every update. Map sharing is neither a closed record layout nor a Sharpurs ownership analysis.
- **15–17**: `let rec … and …` groups, `_tco` entry points and saturated direct calls prepare tail calls for F#/.NET. Sharpurs does not emit a general trampoline/dispatcher that itself guarantees bounded stack usage, or a counted loop. See the [TCO output](../altbak.pub-sharpurs/run/bak/sharp/output/Main/Test.TCO.fs).
- **20–22**: [ThunkKernel](../sharpurs/sharpurs/src/Sharpurs/ThunkKernel.purs) is integrated, including in the bundle and the [LazyEvaluation output](../altbak.pub-sharpurs/run/bak/sharp/output/Main/Test.LazyEvaluation.fs). It specializes private `unit -> int` regions and removes recognized `defer`/`force` helpers. **The computation's closure chain is still built, and the worker is called again on every iteration**: yellow 22, red 20/21.
- **25 (A25)**: the [frontend's newtype desugaring](../purescript/src/Language/PureScript/CoreFn/Desugar.hs) omits a data wrapper and emits an identity constructor; Sharpurs also removes the constructor pattern. This representation erasure is complete. The generic [Lazy output](../altbak.pub-sharpurs/run/bak/sharp/output/Main/Test.LazyEvaluation.fs) retains the identity and some calls, as does the green-rated [official JS output](run/bak/js/output/Test.LazyEvaluation/index.js). Those residual calls do not justify yellow for data-wrapper erasure. The frontend and generic generator provide the representation; PBO additionally removes residual identity applications on accepted optimized paths.
- **27**: `Main` deliberately filters out foreign Effect/ST semantics. No general elimination of local cells in the kernels.
- The experimental `AdtInterop` module is not imported by `Main`: its test capabilities are not attributed to the normal pipeline.
- **30–33**: the accepted optimized kernels/wrappers consume the shared PBO transformations. The generic original-TAST path does not. Ordinary single-field DU cases, boxed local lambdas, and `Map` records remain there: no class-specific dictionary layout, wrapper erasure, nullable-sum selection, or static capture-free function variant (35–39).
- **34, 43–44**: [uncurried FFI](../sharpurs/sharpurs-functions/src/Data/Function/Uncurried.fs) keeps existing curried function values; generic codegen provides no native first-class FnN ABI. Recursive functions keep lexical captures, rather than passing them to a lifted worker. F# value-producing matches already retain one downstream continuation (gray 44).
- **Runtime boundary**: `Main.fsPrelude` first tests for `obj -> obj` and invokes it directly, retaining reflection for other callable objects. [Runtime application tests](../sharpurs/sharpurs/tests/runtime-apply.mjs) cover that production prelude. This is a real runtime dispatch shortcut, recorded under the closure/application discussion rather than credited as a separate compiler pass.

## purust

- **01–05, 18, 25**: [Main](../purust/purust/src/Main.purs) actually passes `backendMod` to the generator. [TypeApp tests](../purust/purust/tests/tast/type-instantiation.mjs) verify the emission of `i64`/`f64` loops after instantiation and the `UnknownType` fallback. There is no general pass that creates clones per instantiation: yellow 05 does not imply yellow 06.
- **07–10, 22, 26**: [CodeGen](../purust/purust/src/Purust/CodeGen.purs), direct calls to globals with known arity, partial applications and overapplications, primitive parameters/returns, fields derived from `dataDecls`, type classes represented as structs. Recursive ADTs use `Rc`, and type variables remain generic. `FuncN::Static` / `Shared` distinguishes functions without captures from shared closures. Neither `FuncN` nor the later monomorphization of Rust generics constitutes the PureScript cloning described in row 06.
- **11 (matrix B39)**: two coverage levels share this yellow cell. [DataLayout](../purust/purust/src/Purust/DataLayout.purs) selects entirely nullary enums from `dataDecls` across all modules and makes them `Copy`; this native enum path is fully integrated, including FFI representation agreement. For mixed ADTs, [ShareNullaries](../purust/purust/src/Purust/ShareNullaries.purs) shares nullary constructors in local strict constructions and reuses a value established by a successful tag test. These rewrites stop at scope/call boundaries; the mixed-ADT coverage remains partial, without a universal global singleton.
- **12–13 (matrix B19/B21)**: record structs are based on label sets, but their fields are **`Option<UnknownType>`**. Getters/setters dispatch on shape. The layout is partially structured, and numeric record fields are not yet unboxed.
- **14 (matrix B20, green)**: `CodeGen` lowers `Update` to fixed-field `set_<label>` methods. Each setter dispatches on the record shape and uses `PerceusPtr::make_mut`: mutate when unique, copy the struct otherwise. The fallback `Record_a` uses the same fixed-field strategy. ## Wasm

- [Runner](bin/wasm/run): 14 core cases, Node ≥22, standard frontend **0.15.16**, import checks that prohibit replacing algorithms with JS. This backend uses **its own MIR**, not our PBO or our TAST. It nevertheless uses externs and representation inference: the red ratings for 04/05 do not mean “no types available”.
- **01–03, 18, 22, 25**: [MiddleEnd](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/MiddleEnd.purs), [DictElim](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/MiddleEnd/Optimize/DictElim.purs), [Semantics](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/MiddleEnd/Optimize/Semantics.purs), [Impurify](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/MiddleEnd/Optimize/Impurify.purs), reachability. **`DictElim.useNbE = true` selects normalization by evaluation; legacy Simplify-only rules receive no credit.** [Specialize](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/MiddleEnd/Optimize/Specialize.purs) creates workers for a known function argument that remains invariant during recursion, with explicit captures; this is not monomorphization of ForAll types.
- **07–10, 26**: [Lower](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/Lower.purs), `RCallKnown` and direct constructors; [Unbox](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/Lower/Unbox.purs), fixed-point signatures, i32/f64 locals, an intermodule/generic ABI that retains boxes. [Collect](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/Lower/Collect.purs) uses externs for ADT fields, with a boxed fallback.
- **11–14, 23**: [Codegen](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/Codegen.purs), entirely nullary enums as i31ref, ADT GC structs, records as parallel label/value arrays with key-based lookup, generic eqref arrays. **Nullary sharing also covers mixed ADTs**: `nullaryTags`/`addNullaryGlobals` creates a global for each used fieldless tag, and `genRhs` reads it. Both regular and per-module generation invoke this path, hence green 11; cross-module pointer identity is not promised. Known `copyFields` allows explicit record reconstruction, without an unboxed numeric payload. [Intrinsics](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/Intrinsics.purs) exposes explicit i32/i64 arrays, without general `Array Int` specialization.
- **15–17**: `return_call` for direct tail calls with a compatible return representation, including between mutually recursive top-level functions. Tail calls through `RApply` remain excluded. No dedicated counter canonicalization pass was identified.
- **19–21, 24, 27**: `SShared` sharing and [CAF planning](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/Codegen/Caf.purs) for existing arity-zero program functions with storable results outside cycles. This does not extract arbitrary closed local aggregates into new globals; cross-module CAF calls can recompute. Neither mechanism constitutes Javapurs's per-invocation cache. No dedicated thunk producer fusion, ownership of unique values, or elimination of Ref cells: [Prim](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/Codegen/Prim.purs) retains the GC RefNew/Read/Write helpers.
- **29**: [DictElim](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/MiddleEnd/Optimize/DictElim.purs) selects `Semantics.normalize` with `useNbE = true`. [Semantics](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/MiddleEnd/Optimize/Semantics.purs), `evalCase` / `selectAlt` / `matchSem`, reduces a case whose constructors are known and substitutes their fields. This reduction is partial and specific to the Wasm MIR; it does not constitute general fusion through an opaque producer.
- **30–32**: `Semantics` projects known record fields and folds updates of known records, under purity/usage guards. Unknown bases remain `NUpdate`/`NAccessor`; there is no equivalent general array scalar replacement. Quoting `SLam` still emits an abstraction, with no dedicated eta rule. A forwarder specialization test that succeeds through beta-reduction/inlining does not prove eta elimination.
- **33, 43–44**: [Lower.Match](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/Lower/Match.purs) groups constructors, shares field occurrences and discards rows after an unguarded catch-all; it chooses the leftmost refutable column, not a globally optimal heuristic. [LambdaLift](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/MiddleEnd/Optimize/LambdaLift.purs) lifts supported self/mutual local recursive function groups, prepending captures; mixed value groups remain. [Lower](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/src/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/Lower.purs) creates a `LetJoin` result slot for argument-position cases, and `Codegen` emits one continuation. [Existing Lower tests](../purescript-backend-wasm/compiler/test/Unit/PureScript/Backend/Wasm/Lower.purs) check shared outer constructor tests and a single continuation call.
- **34–42, 45–46**: FnN intrinsics keep unary `Clo` values and `applyClo` chains. Every residual `RMkClosure` allocates an environment array and closure struct, including empty captures. No class-specific dictionary layout, ordinary-data erasure, nullable/value-result payload layout, static capture-free variant, collection compiler fusion, unchanged-constructor bypass, or common-tail factoring was found. The array/foldable source-library shadows expose algorithms to ordinary HOF specialization; their loops are library implementations, not traversal-to-loop compiler intrinsics.

### Checked without adding duplicate or speculative rows

- **General tail recursion modulo cons/constructor**: no integrated destination-passing/hole-patching transform was established in these pipelines. Native TCO handles calls already in tail position; Rust's nonrecursive return-cell workers do not establish this capability.
- **Full laziness / global hoisting of closed local aggregates**: no general extraction pass was established. Frontend synthetic-dictionary CSE is 19. Sharing already declared globals varies: Go uses `sync.Once`, Java uses static values/guarded getters, Sharp/JS/ES/Scheme have module bindings, PHP initializes globals, psgo caches selected literal objects, and Wasm plans selected existing CAFs. Rust zero-arity bindings can remain functions. These facts do not establish row 20 or a local-expression hoister.
- **General bounds-check elimination, loop strength reduction and arithmetic CSE**: no separate general backend pass was established. Constant folding, direct primitives, unsafe-index APIs and Java's counted-loop recognition are already 01/08/17. Host compilers may optimize further; that is not credited here.
- **General defunctionalization or map/filter/fold pipeline fusion**: specialized closures, strict thunk workers, traversal intrinsics and append flattening are credited in 21–22/39–41 with their actual scope. None establishes those broader transformations.
- **Representation/purity/liveness analysis, conversion and copy cleanup**: these matter, but their concrete outcomes already have rows. Compiler-only memoization, build caching, names/hygiene, metadata transport, dead scratch files, unused modules and handwritten native benchmark shortcuts are excluded.

This section records the negative checks without growing the main matrix into an unbounded wishlist. Future mechanisms should either extend an existing row's evidence or receive a distinct row when their outcome differs.
